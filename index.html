ETF Selection: KPI & Evaluation Framework

A comprehensive reference for evaluating equity, factor, income, international, commodity and bond ETFs.

Prepared October 2026 • Personal investing reference
How to use this report

Use the framework as a research checklist, not as a mechanical buy/sell signal. Compare trends, peers, expectations and valuation; investigate disagreements rather than averaging them away.

ETF evaluation architecture

Evaluate an ETF in this order:

ObjectiveIndex/strategyPortfolioCostTrackingLiquidityRiskTaxPerformancePortfolio fit

The key question is not simply “Which ETF performed best?” but “Which ETF gives me the desired exposure most efficiently and reliably?”

Costs and implementation

  • Expense ratio: recurring operating cost.
  • Tracking difference: ETF return minus benchmark return.
  • Tracking error: standard deviation of ETF-vs-index return differences.
  • Bid-ask spread: immediate trading cost.
  • Slippage: difference between expected and actual execution.
  • Turnover: portfolio trading intensity; relevant to friction and taxes.
  • Securities-lending revenue: can offset some fund costs.
  • Cash drag: cash held by the fund can create small tracking differences.
Total practical cost ≈ Expense Ratio + Tracking Difference + Spread + Slippage + Tax Cost

Index and portfolio construction

  • Index provider: S&P, MSCI, FTSE Russell, CRSP, Nasdaq, proprietary, etc.
  • Methodology: understand eligibility, profitability screens, liquidity rules, rebalancing and reconstitution.
  • Weighting: market-cap, equal-weight, factor, fundamental, dividend, volatility, etc.
  • Holdings count: useful but insufficient without concentration.
  • Top-10 concentration: sum of top ten weights.
  • Largest holding: identifies single-name dependence.
  • HHI: Σ weight²; higher means more concentration.
  • Effective holdings: 1 / Σ weight².

Exposure and factor KPIs

  • Sector weights and sector concentration.
  • Country/geographic exposure.
  • Market-cap exposure: mega/large/mid/small.
  • Style: value/blend/growth.
  • Factor exposure: value, momentum, quality, size, low volatility, profitability, dividend.
  • Weighted-average P/E, forward P/E, P/B, P/S, EV/EBITDA, P/FCF.
  • Weighted earnings growth, ROE/ROIC, margins and leverage.

For international ETFs distinguish listing location from revenue exposure and understand currency hedging.

Liquidity and ETF mechanics

  • AUM: scale and closure-risk indicator, not a return predictor.
  • Average daily volume: useful but not the whole liquidity story.
  • Underlying liquidity: often more important than ETF share volume.
  • NAV: (assets − liabilities) / shares.
  • Premium/discount to NAV: (market price − NAV) / NAV.
  • Creation/redemption: helps keep market price close to NAV.
  • Replication: full replication vs sampling.
  • Fund age and closure risk: older/larger funds provide more implementation evidence.

Valuation and fundamental profile

ETF-level valuation metrics help determine what you're actually paying for the underlying portfolio:

  • Forward P/E and historical P/E range
  • Earnings yield
  • Price/book
  • Price/sales
  • EV/EBITDA
  • FCF yield where available
  • Expected earnings/revenue growth
  • Weighted ROE/ROIC for quality strategies
  • Dividend yield and distribution growth for income strategies
Earnings Yield = 1 / P/E

Compare today's valuation with the ETF's own history and with genuinely comparable ETFs, not arbitrary categories.

Income and bond ETF KPIs

Dividend/income ETFs

  • Dividend yield
  • 3/5/10-year distribution growth
  • Payout ratio and FCF payout of holdings
  • Quality/profitability of holdings
  • Return of capital
  • Covered-call percentage and option methodology for option-income funds

Bond ETFs

  • 30-day SEC yield
  • Yield to maturity
  • Yield to worst
  • Effective duration
  • Average maturity
  • Credit quality distribution
  • Credit spreads
  • Weighted-average coupon
% price change ≈ −Duration × change in yield

Specialized ETF risks

  • Commodity ETFs: physical vs futures structure, collateral yield and roll yield.
  • Contango: futures roll can reduce returns.
  • Backwardation: roll can add returns.
  • Leveraged/inverse ETFs: daily reset means long-term return is not simply 2× or 3× the index return; volatility drag matters.
  • Covered-call ETFs: assess total return, upside sacrificed, option coverage, NAV behavior and distribution composition—not just yield.
  • International ETFs: currency exposure, local-market hours, hedging costs and country concentration.

Portfolio-level ETF analysis

ETF diversification must be assessed across the portfolio, not one fund at a time.

  • Holdings overlap
  • Weighted overlap
  • Sector overlap
  • Factor overlap
  • Country overlap
  • Correlation
  • Marginal contribution to portfolio risk
Example: VOO + QQQ + a large-cap growth ETF can look like three diversified ETFs while creating substantial overlapping mega-cap technology exposure.

ETF's job in the portfolio

Every ETF should have a clear one-sentence job:

  • Core equity diversification
  • Growth tilt
  • Value tilt
  • Small-cap exposure
  • International diversification
  • Bonds/income/volatility control
  • Dividend strategy
  • Sector/tactical exposure
  • Thematic satellite
  • Commodity/inflation diversification

If you cannot explain why the ETF belongs in the portfolio, don't buy it simply because its historical chart looks good.

  7. Portfolio overlap

  12. Earnings growth
  13. 5/10-year total-return CAGR
  14. Maximum drawdown
  15. Sharpe/Sortino

For bond ETFs, prioritize SEC yield, YTM, YTW, duration, credit quality and spread instead of relying on equity-style metrics.

Five questions before buying

  1. What exactly does it own? Read the methodology and actual holdings.
  2. Why does it improve my portfolio? Identify the exposure or risk it adds.
  3. Is there a cheaper/better implementation? Compare close substitutes.
  4. How much overlap does it have with what I already own?
  5. What could make it underperform for 5–10 years? Know the failure mode before buying.
